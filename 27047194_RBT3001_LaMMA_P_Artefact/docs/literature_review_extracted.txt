Abstract 
This project develops a hybrid planning framework that fuses the semantic reasoning 
power of Large Language Models (LLMs) with the structured rigour of the Planning 
Domain Definition Language (PDDL) to achieve explainable coordination at scale of 
multiple robots. Using the LaMMA-P architecture from Zhang et al. (2025), it will 
translate natural-language instructions into PDDL problems, generate executable plans 
through Fast Downward, and execute them collaboratively on simulated and physical 
LIMO robots. 
In this novel contribution, it extends the LLM's preliminary task allocations by a 
constraint-driven optimisation layer using a Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) 
solver. The optimiser maximizes global utility-minimizing travel cost while respecting 
availability, temporal, and capability constraints-thus producing stable, high-quality 
allocations. In the end, this ROS 2-based artefact will demonstrate robust natural-
language coordination in both Gazebo simulation and real-world operation.

1 Introduction 
 Most modern applications, such as logistics, agriculture, and service automation, 
require the effective coordination of multiple autonomous robots, which remains one of 
the most important challenges in robotics. While classical symbolic planners-like those 
described in PDDL (McDermott et al., 1998; Helmert, 2006)-provide logical correctness, 
they have limited flexibility when ambiguity or high-level human instructions arise. On 
the other hand, Large Language Models exhibit remarkable semantic reasoning 
capabilities but produce plans that provide no formal guarantees of feasibility or 
optimality. 
LaMMA-P (Zhang et al. 2025) recently bridged this divide by applying an LLM to break 
down natural-language tasks into symbolic goals, while using Fast Downward for low-
level planning. In the MAT-THOR benchmark, the approach increased task success by 
105 % and efficiency by 36 % over previous LM-based planners. Such results attest to 
the potential value of combining statistical and symbolic reasoning. Nevertheless, its 
use in simulation was limited, and its reliance on probabilistic text generation for task 
allocation led to instability and ill-formatted assignments at times. This level of 
randomness simply cannot guarantee consistent performance, especially when robots 
have very different locations, battery levels, or task loads-as is common in real systems. 

--- PAGE 2 ---
 To address this limitation, the proposed project will extend LaMMA-P with a constraint-
driven optimisation layer that transforms the LLM's task decomposition into a 
mathematically validated allocation. Instead of executing the LLM's textual plan 
directly, each sub-task is encoded as a decision variable within a MILP formulation. In 
this way, the optimization model enforces constraints on capability feasibility, task 
precedence, and non-overlapping temporal windows, along with resource limits, while 
optimizing an objective function—typically to minimize total makespan, energy cost, or 
travel time, or to balance workload distribution across heterogeneous agents. 
The MILP layer will be implemented in modern, open-source optimisation libraries such 
as SciPy's optimize.milp interface, wrapping the HiGHS solver, or Coin-OR's PuLP , which 
support efficient branch-and-bound and presolve strategies for mixed-integer 
problems. These solvers can handle binary assignment variables (x[i,r] ∈ {0,1}) 
alongside continuous scheduling variables (t[i] ≥ 0), thus enabling the system to return 
deterministic, globally consistent allocations that respect both capability and temporal 
feasibility. Using a formal solver also avoids the "length/randomness → wrong format" 
instability that Zhang et al. (2025) identify in the LLM's textual plans, ensuring 
reproducibility and convergence to a mathematically optimal or near-optimal solution. 
Alternative approaches to multi-robot coordination have included auction-based and 
constraint-programming formulations (Dias et al., 2006; Capitan et al., 2013), which 
offer robustness under resource or timing constraints. By integrating these optimisation 
principles with LLM-driven semantic parsing, the proposed system combines the 
generality of language understanding with the reliability of mathematical planning. 
The final architecture will operate within ROS 2 Humble, integrating LLM parsing, PDDL 
problem generation, Fast Downward planning, and MILP allocation before dispatching 
actions through Nav2. Validation will occur first in a Gazebo warehouse world with 
multiple LIMO robots, then on physical LIMO hardware using AprilTag localisation. 
Performance will be compared against PDDL-only and LLM-only baselines on success 
rate, execution efficiency, and utility score. 
This work delivers an embodied demonstration of language-informed symbolic planning 
enriched by optimisation. Practically, it advances reliable natural-language supervision 
of robot fleets, aligning with UN SDG 9 (Industry and Innovation) through intelligent 
automation and improved resource efficiency. 
 
2 Aim 
To design, implement, and evaluate a Large Language Model-driven PDDL planning 
framework enhanced by a constraint-driven optimisation layer that maximises utility 
and ensures reliable multi-LIMO robot coordination. 
SMART Objectives 
1. O1 (Literature & Design) – Critically review LLM-assisted symbolic planners 
(SMART-LLM 2024, LaMMA-P 2025) and define the ROS 2 system architecture. 
2. O2 (PDDL Implementation) – Develop PDDL domain and problem generators 
supporting navigate, pickup, drop, meet, charge actions for multiple robots. 
3. O3 (NL → PDDL Pipeline) – Implement an LLM interface that converts natural-
language commands into structured JSON and valid PDDL problems with 
schema validation. 

--- PAGE 3 ---
4. O4 (Constraint-Driven Optimisation) – Integrate a MILP/ILP module that refines 
LLM-suggested allocations according to robot availability, travel cost, and 
capability constraints, maximising overall system utility. 
5. O5 (Evaluation & Demo) – Benchmark the hybrid planner in Gazebo and on real 
LIMO robots, comparing allocation quality, execution time, and utility gains 
against PDDL-only and LLM-only baselines. 
6. O6 (Reporting) – Document methods and experimental outcomes, producing a 
publication-quality dissertation and demonstration video.
